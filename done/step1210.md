# Step 1210 - Host-only x64 saved-context layout (#183/#713)

## Summary

Implement `X64ThreadContext` for M09-001/#183: a 688-byte unmanaged context
with all sixteen GPRs, RIP/RSP/RFLAGS, FS/user-GS and a 512-byte FXSAVE64
image at offset176. The caller supplies a live, exclusively owned 16-byte-aligned
buffer. Nine fresh focused host checks pass; scoped peer source review found
no actionable defect. #713 owns the exact shared manifest/root-handoff edits.
This is host-only acceptance, with no context transfer or hardware execution.

## Why

The scheduler chain needs a complete, bounded state representation before
queues and actual switching. Existing M16 SYSCALL state transforms RCX/R11 and
is not a full asynchronously interrupted context. A cooperative callee-saved
example is also insufficient. Legal initial FP controls and an independently
trusted MXCSR mask prevent zero-filled or self-authorized state admission.

fields, all scalar widths and FP alignment; legal initialization bytes and
guards; unique GPR/x87/MMX/XMM snapshots; disjoint/self/overlapping copies;
null/short/misaligned/overflowing/noncanonical declared ranges; each declared

All qwords and byte offsets are tabulated in `docs/pal.md`. Header version1
and reserved0 mark completion under exclusive ownership. They do not prove
concurrent publication or permission to restore. RIP/RSP must be nonzero
four-level canonical addresses; FS/user-GS may be zero, both canonical halves
are structurally admitted, and RFLAGS requires bit1 within mask0x00250fd7.
The owner must supply mapped storage even after pointer shape checks pass.
Initial x87 control0x027f and MXCSR0x1f80 are legal Win64 controls. Raw external
MXCSR_MASK0 uses effective0xffbf; saved raw mask must match the trusted input.
Selectors, CR3, kernel-GS, TSS, executable mappings, privilege, stack ABI and
TLS lifetimes remain trusted CPU/transition-owner responsibilities.
