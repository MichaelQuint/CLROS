## What changed and why

- LinuxSocketNetwork selects explicit continuing timer mode. Zero lifetime is
  allowed only with continuing=true; legacy callers retain their fixed lifetime
  and zero-lifetime rejection. Retry and close budgets remain per connection;
  total diagnostic/application budgets belong to the enclosing owner. No
  implicit idle timeout, persist, congestion-control or new protocol is added.
- Actual retry/close exhaustion marks its original sender. RetireTimedOut
  validates that exact faulted transport, closes its table generation without
  synthetic RST or release, and publishes reset-like readiness with distinct
  Linux ETIMEDOUT110. Error reads/SO_ERROR consume it once; failed copy-out
  does not. Other sockets continue. Existing last-close/reap scrubs ownership;
  clock regression, TX-copy and table/provenance corruption still fault the owner.
- Continuing timers reject unrepresentable retry/close deadlines as InvalidClock.
  An accepted TX remains accounted for if its callback advances the clock into
  that failure range; it cannot fabricate immediately elapsed retries.
- ContinuingVirtioNet validates the complete bounded used batch before reading
  content or recycling any descriptor. In-storage unsupported headers, short
  content and oversized/VLAN frames are dropped. A bounded merged discard waits
  for all its buffers and never delivers merged payload. At most eight buffers
  are consumed per call; a following valid single-buffer packet is delivered.
  Out-of-storage lengths, duplicate/invalid IDs, impossible counts, ownership
  and fence failures remain terminal. DroppedFrames counts complete drops.
- The actual device adapter notifies RX whenever the available index advances,
  including Empty or ShortBuffer after preceding drops. Notification failure
  retains faulted DMA ownership for reset. No EFI or runner source changes.
- Refresh only the six exact CRLF-normalized source-admission hashes. The
  whitelist and raw-byte provenance rules remain unchanged.
