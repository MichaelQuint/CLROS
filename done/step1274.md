# Step 1274 - Repeated ThreadPool work, cancellation and in-flight timer drain

## Summary

Implement a separate actual upstream managed stress app for #194's repeated
bounded work, cancellation/completion races and timer teardown. Three Windows
managed rounds complete 24 work items, recover cancelled waits, execute nine
continuations and drain three active callbacks. Seven focused checks across two
fixtures pass; no full-host/native/CLROS QEMU qualification runs. Continue the
same eight-issue batch. Original pinned payload identities remain unchanged.

## Why

The focused ThreadPool payload retains its separate qualified contract. Broader
repeated operations and disposal during a live callback need a distinct app and
producer identity. Exercise actual stock runtime primitives and verify their
outcomes; add no kernel task engine or synthesized work completion.

## Files changed

- `apps/NativeAotThreadPoolStress/NativeAotThreadPoolStress.csproj`, `Program.cs`:
  C#14/.NET11 workstation-GC app. Each of three rounds queues eight actual
  Task.Run operations through a two-permit semaphore. Two operations must be
  simultaneously pending before release; all results, checksum204, unique bit
  mask255, ThreadPool context, zero active work and returned permits are checked.
  This measures app operations, not parallel hardware execution.
- Cancel before work/wait scheduling and during incomplete semaphore/delay
  operations, require original cancellation tokens and final canceled states,
  then recover/release a real semaphore permit. Competing result/cancel
  publishers must have exactly one winner; before/racing/after continuations
  execute once with correct ThreadPool/inline policy and duplicate publication
  attempts fail. Both legitimate result and cancellation outcomes are validated.
- Dispose an inactive timer, then dispose another while its actual callback is
  held on an event. Disposal must remain incomplete until release. Await callback
  drain, mark disposal, then observe a separate real Task.Delay interval with
  no stale or inactive-timer callbacks. Both 50-ms requests require measured
  intervals of at least 25 ms under the existing timer-granularity tolerance;
  post-disposal timing begins at its own `drained` timestamp.
- Operations/stages use 20-second limits and cleanup releases/drains owned gates,
  tasks and callbacks. The process owner independently bounds the whole app at
  90 seconds. Drained app work does not assert runtime worker shutdown.
- `NativeAotThreadPoolStressGuest.cs`: bounded strict whole fourteen-row output,
  work/cancellation/publication inventory, canonical numeric fields, monotonic
  timer/frequency ordering and distinct due/drain/observation timestamps. Strict
  UTF8 file reader releases read-only handles and grants no provenance authority.
- `NativeAotThreadPoolStressTests.cs`: independent valid and malformed/missing/
  reordered/overflow/false-completion/early-clock/stale-callback vectors, bad
  files, actual bounded Windows managed execution and invalid arguments. Explicit
  original-selector rejection prevents premature prepared-program admission.
- Canonical app-model and PAL docs. No original app, qualifier, package/index,
  native source, kernel task behavior or guest selector changes.
